# Batch Prep Notes

## Reality check on timing
Plan for roughly **double** whatever a batch-cook session looks like on paper — even split across two people or multitasked with other cooking. Sheet-pan and one-pot steps overlap less than they seem to; ovens have one capacity, and stovetop steps still need attention one at a time. Don't schedule a batch session back-to-back with other plans.

Given that, it's usually more sustainable to batch **one or two** components at a time rather than a full week's worth in one sitting — see `journal.md` for what's actually in rotation.

## What holds up as a batch vs. what to cook fresh

**Batches well (make once, use across several meals):**
- Cooked chicken breast (plain, seasoned simply) — slice or shred, keeps 4-5 days
- Plain cooked ground turkey (no sauce yet, so it stays flexible) — keeps 4 days
- Hard-boiled eggs — keeps 7 days unpeeled
- Roasted vegetable trays (broccoli, Brussels sprouts, asparagus, zucchini) — keeps 4-5 days, good hot or cold
- Cauliflower mash — keeps 4-5 days, reheats well
- Chia pudding — keeps 4-5 days

**Cook fresh each time (doesn't hold up as leftovers):**
- Salmon, shrimp, and white fish — all overcook or turn rubbery reheated; they're also quick (10-15 min), so there's little to gain from batching them anyway
- Anything with zucchini noodles — goes watery if it sits

## Frozen vegetables
Frozen broccoli and cauliflower work fine for roasting and mashing — pat frozen broccoli dry before roasting so it browns instead of steaming. Frozen Brussels sprouts roast less crisply than fresh (they hold more water); if crisp edges matter, buy fresh for those specifically.
